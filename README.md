## 1) Research Focus and Theoretical Positioning
The diffusion of generative AI has increased the use of credit- or token-based pricing in SaaS, where usage events (e.g., enrichments, generations, automations) are mapped to billable units. This pricing form can improve entry flexibility but may also increase perceived complexity, billing uncertainty, and fairness concerns in algorithmic decision environments (Shin et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2022). At the same time, vendors face margin volatility and pressure to redesign monetization models for faster and more stable value capture (McKinsey Global Institute, 2023; McKinsey & Company, 2025a, 2025b), while broader generative-AI evidence suggests strong productivity potential when adoption conditions are well designed (Al Naqbi et al., 2024; Brynjolfsson et al., 2025).

This thesis studies how token-pricing design choices shape **(a)** adoption-oriented evaluations, **(b)** retention risk appraisals, and **(c)** payback-efficiency mechanisms. The theoretical contribution is an integrated framework linking tariff design and information architecture to customer cognitive appraisals and downstream behavioral risk indicators, grounded in contemporary evidence on AI-enabled work adoption, trust, and fairness in algorithmic markets (Brynjolfsson et al., 2025; Das et al., 2023; Gu et al., 2024; Shin et al., 2022).

## 2) Main Research Question and Sub-Questions
### Main Research Question (MRQ)
How do token-based pay-per-use pricing designs in AI-enabled SaaS influence adoption and retention risk, and which design configurations best balance user acceptance with payback-efficiency mechanisms?

### Sub-Questions
1. **SQ1 (Market Typology):** Which token-pricing archetypes can be identified in AI-enabled SaaS?
2. **SQ2 (Design → Adoption):** How are design attributes (complexity, transparency, predictability controls, commitment mechanisms) associated with adoption-relevant perceptions and market signals?
3. **SQ3 (Design → Retention Risk):** How are these attributes associated with retention-risk narratives in public discourse (e.g., bill shock, confusion, perceived unfairness)?
4. **SQ4 (Causal Mechanisms):** Under cost-spike scenarios, do transparency and predictability controls reduce switching intention via perceived predictability and fairness?
5. **SQ5 (Illustrative Synthesis):** How do illustrative market examples extend or boundary-condition market-level patterns?

---

## 3) Expected Theoretical Contribution
This study aims to contribute by:
- Disaggregating “usage-based pricing” into observable token-design dimensions rather than treating it as one homogeneous model.
- Integrating adoption and retention perspectives within one cognitive-appraisal framework.
- Clarifying what can and cannot be inferred about payback from public design artifacts when internal CAC and margin data are unavailable.

These contributions build on current research in algorithmic fairness, perceived betrayal under discriminatory pricing, and AI-enabled adoption/productivity effects in real work settings (Brynjolfsson et al., 2025; Das et al., 2023; Shin et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2022).

## 4) Conceptual Model and Internal Consistency
### Independent Variables (Pricing Design)
- **Meter complexity:** number of billable meters, conditionality, feature gating, and multipart tariff structure.
- **Transparency:** clarity of token rules, consumption definitions, worked examples, and visibility of pricing logic.
- **Predictability controls:** estimator, dashboard, budget thresholds, alerts, spend caps.
- **Commitment mechanisms:** included token bundles, minimum spend, prepaid packs, annual commitment, rollover policy.
- **Entry friction:** sales gating, credit-card requirement, trial constraints, public-pricing visibility.

### Mediators (Cognitive Appraisals)
- Perceived complexity
- Perceived spend predictability (budgetability)
- Perceived fairness/exploitation risk
- Trust

### Outcomes
- **Adoption-side:** adoption intention (experiment), top-of-funnel proxy indicators (market discourse/interest indicators).
- **Retention-risk side:** switching intention under cost spike (experiment), retention-risk discourse rates.
- **Payback-efficiency side:** observable monetization-stabilization mechanisms (commitment structures, speed-to-paid signals).

### Core Expectations
- Higher complexity and lower transparency increase perceived uncertainty and unfairness, raising retention risk (Shin et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2022).
- Predictability controls attenuate these effects by improving perceived procedural fairness and trust in AI-mediated pricing environments (Das et al., 2023; Shin et al., 2022).
- Commitment mechanisms can improve payback efficiency but may increase adoption friction unless paired with strong transparency and predictability supports (Boston Consulting Group, 2024; McKinsey & Company, 2025b).

This model is internally consistent if payback is treated as **mechanism-level inference** (not realized firm-level CAC payback performance).

## 5) Methodological Design: Feasibility and Validity
### 5.1 Mixed-Method Components
1. **Market Artifact Dataset (N=45 products):** code token-pricing design features with dated snapshots.
2. **Public Discourse Dataset (target >= 5,000 items):** measure normalized risk narratives by product.
3. **Survey Experiment (N=180 minimum; ideally 240–300):** vignette-based manipulation of transparency/predictability controls (and optional commitment factor).
4. **Optional Interviews (6–12):** mechanism plausibility and boundary conditions.

This mixed-method design is aligned with the objective of integrating behavioral evidence, market discourse, and managerial monetization evidence across peer-reviewed and practice-oriented sources (Brynjolfsson et al., 2025; Deloitte Insights, 2024; McKinsey & Company, 2024).

### 5.2 Feasibility Assessment
- The design is feasible if coding rules are strict, snapshot windows are fixed, and product-level inclusion thresholds are pre-registered.
- Interviews should remain optional to avoid jeopardizing completion.
- A staged plan (artifact coding → discourse validation → survey pilot → fielding) is methodologically sound.

### 5.3 Validity Risks and Mitigation
- **Construct validity risk:** token features are multidimensional.
  **Mitigation:** explicit codebook, double-coding, inter-rater checks.
- **Measurement validity risk (text):** classifier false positives.
  **Mitigation:** human-labeled benchmark, precision/recall reporting.
- **Causal validity risk:** experiment uses intention outcomes.
  **Mitigation:** clearly delimit claims to attitudinal/intentional effects.
- **External validity risk:** public discourse is non-representative.
  **Mitigation:** treat as risk-signal environment, not direct churn measurement.

The validity architecture is aligned with best-practice principles for fairness-sensitive algorithmic evaluation and consumer-risk interpretation in digital markets (Calvano, 2023; Das et al., 2023; Shin et al., 2022).

## 6) Measurement Model Improvements
- Keep the **Credit Pricing Design Index (CPDI)** as a formative index, but report:
  - dimension-level scores,
  - weighting logic (equal or theoretically justified),
  - robustness checks using alternative weighting schemes.
- For reflective constructs (predictability, fairness, trust):
  - use multi-item validated scales where available,
  - report reliability and discriminant validity checks,
  - include manipulation checks for transparency and predictability salience.
- For discourse metrics:
  - predefine dictionaries/rules,
  - validate on manually coded subsets,
  - report confidence intervals for normalized rates.

## 7) Analysis Plan Improvements
- **SQ1:** typology via transparent rule-based taxonomy first; cluster analysis as robustness.
- **SQ2/SQ3:** association models with category controls and exposure normalization; present as correlational evidence.
- **SQ4:** regression/ANOVA with mediation analysis (predictability and fairness as mediators); test subgroup heterogeneity (e.g., practitioner vs student, high vs low prior AI-tool use).
- **SQ5:** structured illustrative comparison against archetype expectations and boundary conditions.

For payback interpretation:
- avoid claiming realized CAC payback;
- frame findings as design features likely to influence revenue stability and speed-to-paid pathways.

This distinction is necessary because realized payback estimation requires firm-internal cost and cohort data beyond observable external artifacts, even when market-facing monetization trends are clear (Bain & Company, 2025; McKinsey & Company, 2025a; PwC, 2024).

## 8) Missing Constructs, Literature Streams, and Alternative Explanations
The draft should explicitly engage additional constructs and alternatives:
- **Outcome uncertainty** (AI quality variance) as a driver of fairness and retry sensitivity.
- **Reference-price and mental-accounting effects** in token spending.
- **Switching costs / lock-in** as a rival explanation for retention-risk narratives.
- **Category maturity and buyer sophistication** as moderators.
- **Product performance quality** (not just pricing design) as a competing explanation for negative discourse.

Recommended literature expansion should include:
- pricing fairness and complexity perceptions,
- usage-based / nonlinear tariff design,
- post-adoption resistance and trust in digital services,
- B2B onboarding and contracting frictions under hybrid monetization.

These streams are theoretically consistent with evidence on fairness perceptions, trust-based AI adoption, and algorithmic market harms under opaque pricing or decision systems (Calvano, 2023; Das et al., 2023; Gu et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2022).

## 9) Limits of Inference (Clarified)
- No claim of firm-level CAC payback performance without internal CAC, gross margin, and cohort data.
- No direct churn inference from public discourse.
- Experimental results identify causal effects on perceptions/intentions under designed scenarios, not observed long-run behavior.
- Illustrative examples are interpretive aids, not the sole evidentiary base.

## 10) Revised Academic-Tone Abstract
This thesis examines token-based pay-per-use pricing in AI-enabled SaaS and asks how tariff design influences adoption, retention risk, and payback-efficiency mechanisms. Recent evidence shows that generative AI can increase workplace productivity while also intensifying concerns about fairness, opacity, and consumer harm in algorithmic systems, making pricing design a strategic and governance-critical issue (Al Naqbi et al., 2024; Brynjolfsson et al., 2025; Calvano, 2023; Shin et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2022). To address this gap, the study combines a market artifact dataset (N=45), public discourse analysis, a vignette-based survey experiment, and optional interviews, and triangulates findings with industry evidence on AI monetization and software pricing redesign (Boston Consulting Group, 2024, 2025; Deloitte Insights, 2024; McKinsey Global Institute, 2023; McKinsey & Company, 2024, 2025a, 2025b). The final objective is to deliver an actionable, theory-informed framework that helps researchers and SaaS decision-makers design token-pricing systems that improve adoption quality, reduce retention-risk drivers, and support sustainable monetization under AI-driven uncertainty.
